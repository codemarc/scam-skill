# Patterns and practical next steps

Use patterns as reasoning aids, not verdict rules. Explain the requested action and potential loss. Multiple weak signals do not automatically equal strong evidence.

| Pattern | Evidence to look for | Independent check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impersonation or phishing | Claimed organization conflicts with destination; password or one-time-code request | Open the known official app or type its independently verified address |
| Easy-money or AI income pitch | Extraordinary earnings, little work, missing business mechanism, seller-controlled testimonials | Request documented costs and substantiation; check official consumer warnings |
| Task or withdrawal-fee scam | Fake balance grows, then a deposit, tax, or upgrade is demanded to withdraw | Stop paying; contact the actual payment provider through its official channel |
| Fake job or check | Pay to get hired, buy equipment from a nominated vendor, deposit a check and forward money | Verify the vacancy with the employer's independently located recruiting team |
| Tech support or remote access | Unsolicited alarm, call-now demand, software installation or account transfer | Contact the product's official support through a known channel |
| Relationship or emergency impersonation | New payment request, secrecy, changed account, voice or video used as sole proof | Call the person on a previously known number |
| Invoice or payment diversion | Changed bank details or urgent executive request | Confirm with the known vendor or colleague through an established channel |
| Recovery scam | Upfront fee or recovery phrase demanded to recover earlier losses | Work through the actual bank, platform, or official reporting channel |

## URL interpretation

Read the hostname rather than a logo or display name. In `bank.example.attacker.invalid`, the presence of `bank` does not make it the bank. In `trusted.invalid@attacker.invalid`, the host is `attacker.invalid`. Watch for lookalike characters and encoded or clipped destinations. Do not guess the registrable domain for unfamiliar public suffixes. A visible shortened URL does not disclose its final destination. A real vendor domain does not authenticate every tenant or page on it.

Never make a suspect URL clickable in the answer. Show the hostname in code or defang it. Use clickable links only for independently established official verification sources.

## If something already happened

Choose steps based on what actually happened, not every hypothetical exposure.

| Exposure | Prioritize |
| --- | --- |
| Only viewed a page | Close it; viewing alone is not evidence of a compromise |
| Shared a password or code | Use the real service from a trusted device; change affected or reused passwords and review sessions and recovery settings |
| Sent money or card details | Contact the bank or payment provider promptly through its known official number or app; ask about stopping or disputing the transaction without promising recovery |
| Installed software or granted remote access | End remote access and disconnect the affected device; use another trusted device to contact official support and secure affected accounts |
| Shared identity information | Use the relevant country's official identity-theft guidance; do not request additional sensitive details in chat |

Preserve relevant receipts and messages privately. Offer reporting guidance if relevant. Never publish victims' data or promise that law enforcement, a bank, or a tool will recover funds.
